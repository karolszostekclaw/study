GitHub (private study repo)

How to make the token
  1. On GitHub: Settings → Developer settings → Personal access tokens → Fine-grained tokens → Generate new token.
  2. Repository access: Only select repositories → karol-learning.
  3. Permissions → Repository permissions → Contents: Read and write. Nothing else.
  4. Pick an expiry you are happy to renew (90 days is a fair default), generate, and paste it here.

Claude (hints, marking, Ask)

About the key
  1. Create it at console.anthropic.com → API keys. API use is billed there, separately from a Claude subscription.
  2. Set a monthly spend limit in the Console. A marking is usually a few cents; Ask and hints cost less.
  3. The key is sent only to api.anthropic.com, straight from this device.
How to use the app

Feed: swipe through cards. Got it pushes a card further out (1, 3, 7, 16 days…); Again brings it back in a few cards. Ask explains a card or tutors you through it.

Study: choose which topics show in the Feed, read a topic in order, or sit a 5-question timed mini exam.

Papers: one paper a week, in order. Get 3 questions to read with, a section-by-section guide (attach the PDF for a better one), then explain the paper back and get it checked. Notes save to papers/<paper>/ in your repo.

Desk → Exams: the monthly checkpoint. Prepare it while you're online, then sit it offline: 90 minutes, closed book. Hand in your pages and each question is marked; anything you score 0–1 comes back next time.

Plan: the whole curriculum as weeks with dates. Each week has a Mon–Thu book unit, a Saturday unit (coding, or the monthly checkpoint), a work-time item and any to-dos. Tick things off as you go. Progress (top of the tab) turns the ticks into bars and a checklist you can screenshot, share as an image or copy as text to share or paste into a learning app; notes, solutions, promo items and to-dos stay out. Behind? Delay a day or Delay a week moves this week and everything after it back; breaks stay put, and you can undo. The plan comes from scripts/build_schedule.py in your repo.

Desk: sit-down exercise sessions.

Drill: timed practice from a catalog in your private repo. Today shows what's learned, re-tests that are due and what to do next. The drills themselves happen on a laptop with the drill tool (drill/README.md in your repo): blank file, no autocomplete, timed and tested; each attempt syncs here. A drill is learned after two first-run passes within its target time on different days, then comes back after 30 days, 90 days and every 6 months. Drills lists them all with the task, the interface and your times, and you can log an attempt done on paper. Units is for the rest of the course: tick parts off, and log mock scores out of 5.

  1. Desk → pick the chapter → pick a problem. Anchors count towards the chapter score; extras are practice. "What you need" is free to read.
  2. Tap Start and work on paper or in GoodNotes, with the problem number at the top of the page. After 25 minutes you can ask for a hint, which caps that problem at 2/3.
  3. Finished? In GoodNotes: Share → Export → Image, saved to Photos or Files. On paper: take a photo.
  4. Under Mark now, choose your pages in order and tap Mark my answer. You need a connection for this.
  5. Read the mark, add a one-line post-mortem, and dispute it if it's wrong.

Everything saves to your private repo on its own: about 20 seconds after you stop, or when you leave the app. Offline, it waits on this device and syncs later. Every Sunday evening the review checks the week's marks and adds a flashcard for anything you scored under 2.
